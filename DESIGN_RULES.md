# GLOBAL DESIGN RULES — AVOID AI-GENERATED LOOK

These rules are mandatory for every UI screen, component, interaction, piece of copy, empty state, dialog, onboarding screen, icon treatment, animation, and visual decision.

The objective is to make the application look like it was designed deliberately by a skilled human product designer for a real production application — NOT generated by an AI UI generator.

Do not optimize for visual novelty, "beautiful AI UI," Dribbble aesthetics, or generic modern-app conventions. Optimize for clarity, restraint, usability, consistency, and intentional design decisions.

> **Exception (2026-11-22, owner's decision):** the app uses the Nightmode glass design system: translucent glass surfaces and the fixed backdrop glow from `ui/Glass.kt` / `ui/Components.kt`, with one blue accent. Glass, backdrop and cards come **only** from those shared components (`AppCard`, `RowCard`, `glassBackdrop`, ...). Never hand-roll new gradients, blurs or glows in a screen. Every other rule below still applies.

---

## 1. ABSOLUTELY AVOID GENERIC AI UI PATTERNS

Do NOT automatically use:

* Purple as the primary accent.
* Blue-purple gradients.
* Neon colors.
* Pink/purple/blue "AI" color palettes.
* Glassmorphism.
* Excessive translucency.
* Excessive blur.
* Floating glowing elements.
* Gradient backgrounds.
* Gradient buttons.
* Gradient borders.
* Giant rounded cards.
* Excessive pill-shaped controls.
* Excessive circular containers.
* Excessive shadows.
* Huge hero sections.
* Decorative blobs.
* Abstract geometric decorations.
* Random floating shapes.
* Sparkles.
* Stars.
* AI sparkle icons.
* Magic-wand icons unless genuinely required by the feature.
* Excessive emoji usage.
* Emoji as a substitute for proper icons.
* Random illustrations.
* Generic 3D illustrations.
* Stock-looking illustrations.
* Fake "AI assistant" aesthetics.
* Excessive use of cards.
* Putting every piece of information inside its own card.
* "Dashboard" layouts when the application does not actually need a dashboard.
* Excessive statistics.
* Fake metrics.
* Decorative progress bars.
* Circular percentage indicators used purely for decoration.
* Giant numbers without a functional purpose.
* Excessive badges.
* Excessive chips.
* Excessive section containers.
* Nested cards.
* Cards inside cards.
* A screen made from many floating rounded rectangles.
* Excessive bottom sheets.
* Excessive modals.
* Excessive tooltips.
* Excessive micro-interactions.
* Bouncy animations everywhere.
* Artificially "playful" UI.
* Generic onboarding with three illustrations and "Get Started."
* Generic "Welcome to X" copy.
* Generic "Unlock your potential" copy.
* Generic "Take control of your…" copy.
* Generic marketing language.

If an element does not serve a clear functional or informational purpose, remove it.

---

## 2. DO NOT MAKE EVERYTHING ROUNDED

Do not treat rounded corners as the default solution for every component.

Avoid a UI where:

* Buttons are rounded.
* Cards are rounded.
* Inputs are rounded.
* Dialogs are rounded.
* Bottom sheets are rounded.
* Containers are rounded.
* Icons sit inside rounded squares.
* Every element has the same corner radius.

Use different shapes only when they have a functional reason.

Prefer:

* Flat surfaces.
* Subtle corner radii.
* Simple dividers.
* Whitespace.
* Typography hierarchy.
* Alignment.
* Natural grouping.

Corner radius should communicate component hierarchy rather than decorate everything.

Avoid extremely large radii unless the component is intentionally pill-shaped.

---

## 3. DO NOT OVERUSE CARDS

One of the strongest signs of generic AI-generated UI is turning every piece of information into a card.

Do not create:

Card → card → card → card → card.

Instead, use:

* Sections.
* Spacing.
* Dividers.
* Lists.
* Simple rows.
* Typography.
* Inline controls.
* Grouped settings.
* Full-width content.

Use a card only when the content genuinely benefits from being visually separated from its surrounding context.

If removing a card makes the UI clearer, remove it.

---

## 4. USE RESTRAINT

The UI should feel calm and intentional.

Do not add visual elements merely because the screen looks "empty."

Whitespace is acceptable.

An empty area does NOT mean something needs to be added.

Do not fill whitespace with:

* Illustrations.
* Icons.
* Decorative shapes.
* Gradients.
* Statistics.
* Quotes.
* Tips.
* Promotional messages.
* Fake activity.
* Extra cards.

A professional application can have large areas of whitespace.

---

## 5. COLOR RULES

Use a restrained color system.

Default approach:

* One primary accent.
* Neutral background.
* Neutral surfaces.
* Neutral text.
* One destructive color.
* One success color when necessary.
* One warning color when necessary.

Do not introduce a new color for every feature.

Do not use color merely to make the screen look interesting.

Avoid:

* Neon colors.
* Highly saturated backgrounds.
* Rainbow palettes.
* Purple/blue AI gradients.
* Multiple competing accent colors.
* Gradient text.
* Gradient icons.
* Gradient buttons.
* Glowing elements.

Colors should communicate hierarchy, state, interaction, or meaning.

---

## 6. TYPOGRAPHY

Typography must feel like real product typography rather than a marketing landing page.

Avoid:

* Huge headings.
* Oversized numbers.
* Excessive bold text.
* ALL CAPS everywhere.
* Extremely thin text.
* Too many font weights.
* Decorative fonts.
* Excessive letter spacing.
* Gradient text.

Use a small, consistent typography scale.

Hierarchy should primarily come from:

1. Size
2. Weight
3. Spacing
4. Position
5. Color

Do not make every heading visually dramatic.

Most application text should be quiet and functional.

---

## 7. CONTENT MUST NOT SOUND AI-GENERATED

Do not write copy that sounds like marketing generated by ChatGPT.

Avoid phrases such as:

* "Unlock your potential"
* "Take your experience to the next level"
* "Seamlessly manage…"
* "Powerful yet simple"
* "Designed for you"
* "Your journey starts here"
* "Supercharge your productivity"
* "Elevate your experience"
* "Discover a smarter way"
* "Revolutionize the way you…"
* "Experience the future"
* "Built to empower you"
* "Everything you need, all in one place"
* "Simple. Powerful. Beautiful."
* "Work smarter, not harder."
* "Say goodbye to…"
* "Meet your new…"
* "The ultimate…"
* "Next-generation…"
* "AI-powered" unless the feature genuinely is AI-powered.

Prefer direct, concrete language.

Bad: "Unlock a smarter way to manage your files."
Good: "Find duplicate files."

Bad: "Take control of your screen experience."
Good: "Dim the screen below the system minimum."

Bad: "Your personalized productivity journey starts here."
Good: "Choose what you want to track."

Every sentence should communicate something useful.

---

## 8. NO FAKE PERSONALIZATION

Do not generate fake personalized content such as:

* "Good morning, Madhav!"
* "Ready to crush your goals?"
* "You're doing great!"
* "Welcome back!"
* "Your day is looking productive!"
* "Keep up the amazing work!"

Unless the application genuinely needs this information and it provides functional value, omit it.

The application should not pretend to know how the user feels.

---

## 9. NO UNNECESSARY EMOJIS

Do not use emojis in normal application UI unless emojis are explicitly part of the product concept.

Avoid:

* Emoji section headings.
* Emoji buttons.
* Emoji feature cards.
* Emoji statistics.
* Emoji onboarding.
* Emoji empty states.
* Emoji notifications.

Use proper icons where an icon is genuinely useful.

---

## 10. ICONS

Icons should be functional, not decorative.

Do not put every icon inside:

* A colored circle.
* A rounded square.
* A gradient container.
* A glowing container.

Avoid unnecessarily complex icons.

Use a coherent icon family throughout the application.

Do not mix:

* Filled icons
* Outlined icons
* 3D icons
* Emoji
* Custom illustrations

without a deliberate reason.

Icon stroke width, size, optical weight, and style must remain consistent.

---

## 11. SPACING

Do not randomly use large spacing to imitate premium design.

Use a consistent spacing system.

Spacing should reflect hierarchy:

* Related items → close together.
* Different groups → more space.
* Major sections → clearly separated.
* Primary action → easy to locate.

Do not make every component float independently.

Elements that belong together should visually belong together.

---

## 12. ALIGNMENT

Everything should appear deliberately aligned.

Maintain consistent:

* Left edges.
* Baselines.
* Icon alignment.
* Button heights.
* Input heights.
* Section spacing.
* Content margins.

Avoid layouts where every element has its own arbitrary width and position.

Do not center-align everything.

Use left alignment for most information-heavy interfaces unless centered alignment serves a clear purpose.

---

## 13. BUTTONS

Do not make every button huge.

Avoid:

* Giant pill buttons.
* Gradient buttons.
* Glowing buttons.
* Multiple competing primary buttons.
* Buttons with unnecessary icons.
* Excessively verbose button labels.

Use concise labels.

Prefer "Scan" instead of "Start Your Smart Scan Experience".

Prefer "Save" instead of "Save Your Changes Now".

Primary actions should be visually obvious without being visually loud.

---

## 14. NAVIGATION

Do not invent navigation patterns simply to make the app look sophisticated.

Use the simplest navigation model that supports the actual information architecture.

Avoid:

* Excessive tabs.
* Tabs for things that are not true categories.
* Navigation items with long labels.
* Decorative navigation.
* Unnecessary floating action buttons.
* Multiple simultaneous navigation systems.

Do not use a bottom navigation bar merely because many modern apps have one.

If the app has only a few core destinations, consider whether a simpler structure is better.

---

## 15. SETTINGS SCREENS

Settings should look like settings.

Do not convert settings into colorful feature cards.

Prefer:

* Clear section labels.
* Simple rows.
* Toggle controls.
* Secondary descriptions.
* Dividers where useful.
* Predictable hierarchy.

Avoid making every setting look like a promotional feature.

GOOD:

    Screen dimming
    Automatically adjust dimming based on the selected schedule.
    [Toggle]

BAD: A large colorful card with an illustration, glowing icon, title, description, badge, and "Enable Now" button.

---

## 16. EMPTY STATES

Do not automatically create a giant illustration.

An empty state should first explain:

1. What is empty.
2. Why it is empty, if relevant.
3. What the user can do next.

Prefer simple typography and a single relevant action.

Avoid:

* Giant illustrations.
* Inspirational quotes.
* Excessive emoji.
* Generic motivational language.

---

## 17. ERROR MESSAGES

Errors must be factual and actionable.

Bad: "Oops! Something went wrong 😢"
Better: "Couldn't load your files. Check storage permission and try again."

Bad: "Uh-oh! We hit a little bump."
Better: "Scan failed. 3 files could not be read."

Explain what happened and what the user can do.

---

## 18. SUCCESS MESSAGES

Do not celebrate every interaction.

Do not show "🎉 Success!" for routine actions.

Use subtle confirmation when appropriate: "Saved." "Scan complete." "Permission granted." "Schedule enabled."

Reserve stronger visual feedback for genuinely important milestones.

---

## 19. ANIMATIONS

Animations should communicate:

* State changes.
* Navigation.
* Spatial relationships.
* Progress.
* Feedback.

Do not animate everything.

Avoid:

* Excessive bouncing.
* Spring animations everywhere.
* Continuous floating.
* Pulsing.
* Shimmering.
* Glowing.
* Parallax for no reason.
* Confetti for routine actions.

If an animation does not improve comprehension, remove it.

Animations should generally be short, subtle, and interruptible.

---

## 20. SHADOWS

Do not give every component a shadow.

Avoid the "floating cards everywhere" look.

Use elevation only when there is an actual hierarchy between surfaces.

Prefer borders, spacing, and contrast when those communicate hierarchy better than shadows.

---

## 21. MODALS AND BOTTOM SHEETS

Do not use a modal for information that can simply exist on the current screen.

Do not use bottom sheets merely because they look modern.

Before creating a modal, ask: "Does the user actually need to temporarily leave the current context?"

If not, keep the information inline.

---

## 22. DON'T CREATE A FAKE DASHBOARD

Do not turn the home screen into:

Greeting → Big number → Three statistics → Four cards → Progress chart → Tip card → Recent activity → Promotional card

unless the product genuinely requires those things.

The home screen should contain the actions and information users actually need.

---

## 23. NO DECORATIVE DATA

Do not create:

* Fake charts.
* Fake statistics.
* Fake percentages.
* Fake activity.
* Fake "insights."
* Fake recommendations.
* Fake progress.

Every displayed number must correspond to real application data.

If there is no meaningful data, show nothing rather than inventing visual content.

---

## 24. PRODUCT-SPECIFIC DESIGN OVER GENERIC DESIGN

Do not start with a generic template and force the product into it.

First understand:

* What the app actually does.
* What the user's primary task is.
* What information matters.
* What actions matter.
* How frequently each action is performed.
* What state the application can be in.

Then design around those facts.

The UI should look like it belongs to THIS application.

It should not look interchangeable with:

* A finance app.
* A habit tracker.
* An AI assistant.
* A productivity app.
* A generic dashboard.
* A template from a UI generator.

---

## 25. REALISTIC PRODUCT COPY

Use language a real product team would actually ship.

Rules:

* Be specific.
* Be concise.
* Use normal human vocabulary.
* Prefer verbs.
* Avoid hype.
* Avoid unnecessary adjectives.
* Avoid marketing language inside functional UI.
* Don't explain obvious things.
* Don't talk to the user like a motivational coach.

Every label should answer: "What does this do?"

Every description should answer: "Why would I care?"

If neither is necessary, remove it.

---

## 26. DO NOT OVER-DESIGN THE ONBOARDING

Do not automatically create:

Screen 1 → illustration, Screen 2 → illustration, Screen 3 → illustration, Screen 4 → permissions, Screen 5 → "You're ready!"

Only introduce onboarding when users actually need explanation.

If the app is self-explanatory, let users start immediately.

Ask for permissions at the point where the permission is required rather than dumping every permission request onto the first launch.

---

## 27. ACCESSIBILITY IS PART OF THE DESIGN

Do not sacrifice usability for aesthetics.

Ensure:

* Adequate contrast.
* Readable text sizes.
* Clear touch targets.
* Visible focus/pressed states.
* Meaningful content descriptions.
* Do not rely only on color to communicate state.
* Controls have understandable labels.
* Text remains readable at larger font sizes.

A UI that looks "minimal" but is difficult to operate is not good design.

---

## 28. VISUAL HIERARCHY

Every screen should have a clear hierarchy.

There should generally be:

1. Primary purpose.
2. Primary information.
3. Primary action.
4. Secondary information.
5. Secondary actions.

Do not give ten elements equal visual importance.

If everything is emphasized, nothing is emphasized.

---

## 29. AVOID DESIGN TRENDS FOR THEIR OWN SAKE

Do not use:

* Glassmorphism.
* Neumorphism.
* Bento grids.
* Floating islands.
* Oversized typography.
* Giant rounded cards.
* Gradient borders.
* Animated gradients.
* AI sparkle motifs.
* "Liquid glass."
* Excessive blur.
* 3D illustrations.
* Excessive depth.
* Decorative noise.

unless the product concept genuinely requires them.

Being modern does not mean using every current UI trend.

---

## 30. HUMAN DESIGN TEST

Before finalizing every screen, evaluate it using these questions:

1. Could this screen have been generated from a generic AI UI prompt?
2. Does every visual element have a reason to exist?
3. Are there too many cards?
4. Are there too many rounded rectangles?
5. Are there too many colors?
6. Is there unnecessary decoration?
7. Does the copy sound like marketing AI?
8. Are emojis being used unnecessarily?
9. Is the screen trying too hard to look "premium"?
10. Would removing an element make the interface clearer?
11. Does the design reflect this specific application's function?
12. Would a real product designer have a concrete reason for each visual decision?

If the answer to any of these indicates unnecessary complexity, simplify the design.

---

## 31. DEFAULT PRINCIPLE

When uncertain between A) more visual decoration and B) less decoration with better hierarchy — choose B.

When uncertain between A) another card and B) whitespace, typography, or a divider — choose B.

When uncertain between A) clever marketing copy and B) direct functional language — choose B.

When uncertain between A) another animation and B) a clear static state — choose B.

When uncertain between A) a trendy UI pattern and B) a conventional pattern that users already understand — choose B.

When uncertain between A) adding something because the screen looks empty and B) leaving the space empty — choose B.

The final interface should feel restrained, purposeful, product-specific, and believable.

DO NOT make the application look like an AI-generated design showcase.

Make it look like a real application that has been iterated on by a product designer and tested by real users.
